Write your own rhetorical analyses

  • Close Reading: A Step-by-Step Guide (Brandeis University) - A four-stage analytical workflow for breaking down syntax, tone, and rhetorical structure in source passages.
  • How to Read a Primary Source (Patrick Rael, Bowdoin College) - A foundational guide to evaluating historical texts through authorial intent, audience, and argumentative construction.
  • Rhetorical Analyses Handout (Miami University Howe Center for Writing Excellence) - A structured reference on evaluating rhetorical appeals (ethos, pathos, logos) and communicative purpose.
  • Close Reading a Text and Avoiding Pitfalls (Purdue OWL) - Practical instruction on annotating patterns, rhetorical devices, and historical assumptions without over-generalizing.

Trace your own conceptual genealogies

  • Theory and Method: Conceptual History (Helge Jordheim, University of Oslo) - A methodological breakdown of Reinhart Koselleck's Begriffsgeschichte and frameworks for tracing semantic shifts over time.
  • Begriffsgeschichte and Social History (Reinhart Koselleck, German History Intersections) - Koselleck's primary methodological text defining the distinction between words and concepts and their sociohistorical force.

Curate your own digital exhibits

  • Up and Running with Omeka.net (Programming Historian) - A peer-reviewed tutorial on creating digital archives, structuring item metadata, and organizing archival collections.
  • Creating an Omeka Exhibit (Programming Historian) - A step-by-step walkthrough for designing narrative pathways, section layouts, and contextual interpretative pages for primary documents.
  • Building a Digital Exhibition with CollectionBuilder and the Internet Archive (Programming Historian) - A workflow for generating metadata-driven static web exhibits using open collections and GitHub.

Annotate your own primary texts

  • 10 Minute Tutorial: Recogito (Pelagios Network) - A guide to semantic annotation, person/place tagging, and relationship-mapping across digitized texts and images.
  • Introduction to Encoding Texts in TEI (Programming Historian) - A practical tutorial on marking up historical documents with XML-based Text Encoding Initiative (TEI) standards.
  • How to Prepare an Annotated Bibliography (Cornell University Library) - Instructions for summarizing, critically evaluating, and establishing the historiographical relevance of primary sources.

Build your own interactive timelines

  • TimelineJS: Make a Timeline (Knight Lab) - An open-source tool and template workflow for converting chronological source spreadsheets into interactive multimedia narratives.
  • Making a Timeline from a Google Spreadsheet (Knight Lab) - Documentation covering date formatting, era spans, media embeds, and event grouping for historical chronology projects.

Record your own history podcasts

  • Starting Your Podcast: A Guide for Students (NPR) - A blueprint for producing narrative audio, covering story structure, scripting, interview techniques, and field recording.
  • Project Audio: Teaching Students How to Produce Their Own Podcasts (The New York Times) - A structured project unit breaking down voiceover narration, audio editing, and sound design workflows.

Publish your own public history essays

  • Writing for Public-Facing Digital History Publications: Some Tips & Tricks (The Panorama) - Guidance from journal editors on translating scholarly arguments into accessible, clear essays for non-academic readers.
  • Writing History for a Popular Audience: A Round Table (The American Historian / OAH) - Practicing historians evaluate narrative craft, evidence presentation, and stylistic clarity outside the academy.

Write your own scholarly book reviews

  • How to Write a History Book Review (University of Iowa History Department) - A guide covering thesis identification, source evaluation, and assessing how a monograph contributes to broader historical debates.
  • Book Reviews (UNC Writing Center) - Structural blueprints and critical questions for evaluating nonfiction arguments and scholarly evidence.
  • How to Write an Academic Book Review (Wendy Laura Belcher) - A professional step-by-step drafting schedule, outlining formula, and evaluation model by an academic editor.
  • How to Write a Critical Book Review (Carleton College History Department) - Specific criteria for examining a historian's methodological approach, use of primary evidence, and underlying interpretive bias.

Draft your own historiographical research papers

  • Reading, Writing, and Researching for History: A Guide for College Students (Patrick Rael, Bowdoin College) - A full guide to the research lifecycle, from interrogating sources and predatory reading to crafting historiographical interventions.
  • Steps for Writing a History Paper (UCLA Department of History) - A sequential workflow moving from topic selection and source interrogation to drafting and thesis argumentation.
  • How to Write a History Research Paper (Carleton College History Department) - Walkthrough on structuring an extended historical research paper, outlining thematic sections, and managing citations.
  • A Brief Guide to Writing the History Paper (Harvard College Writing Center) - Concise guidance on constructing evidence-backed historical claims, evaluating scholarly disagreements, and avoiding anachronism.
