## Write your own rhetorical analyses

- [Close Reading: A Step-by-Step Guide (Brandeis University)](https://www.brandeis.edu/writing-program/undergraduate/resources/student-writing-guides/close-reading.html) - A four-stage analytical workflow for breaking down syntax, tone, and rhetorical structure in source passages.
- [How to Read a Primary Source (Patrick Rael, Bowdoin College)](https://courses.bowdoin.edu/writing-guides/reading/how-to-read-a-primary-source/) - A foundational guide to evaluating historical texts through authorial intent, audience, and argumentative construction.
- [Rhetorical Analyses Handout (Miami University Howe Center for Writing Excellence)](https://miamioh.edu/hcwe/handouts/rhetorical-analyses/index.html) - A structured reference on evaluating rhetorical appeals (ethos, pathos, logos) and communicative purpose.
- [Close Reading a Text and Avoiding Pitfalls (Purdue OWL)](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/writing_in_literature/writing_about_fiction/close_reading_a_text_and_avoiding_pitfalls.html) - Practical instruction on annotating patterns, rhetorical devices, and historical assumptions without over-generalizing.

## Trace your own conceptual genealogies

- [Theory and Method: Conceptual History (Helge Jordheim, University of Oslo)](https://www.hf.uio.no/ikos/english/research/news-and-events/events/phd/2021/jordheim_conceptual-history_bloomsbury.pdf) - A methodological breakdown of Reinhart Koselleck's *Begriffsgeschichte* and frameworks for tracing semantic shifts over time.
- [Begriffsgeschichte and Social History (Reinhart Koselleck, German History Intersections)](https://germanhistory-intersections.org/en/knowledge-and-education/ghis:document-129) - Koselleck's primary methodological text defining the distinction between words and concepts and their sociohistorical force.

## Curate your own digital exhibits

- [Up and Running with Omeka.net (Programming Historian)](https://programminghistorian.org/en/lessons/up-and-running-with-omeka) - A peer-reviewed tutorial on creating digital archives, structuring item metadata, and organizing archival collections.
- [Creating an Omeka Exhibit (Programming Historian)](https://programminghistorian.org/en/lessons/creating-an-omeka-exhibit) - A step-by-step walkthrough for designing narrative pathways, section layouts, and contextual interpretative pages for primary documents.
- [Building a Digital Exhibition with CollectionBuilder and the Internet Archive (Programming Historian)](https://programminghistorian.org/en/lessons/exhibition-with-collectionbuilder-internet-archive) - A workflow for generating metadata-driven static web exhibits using open collections and GitHub.

## Annotate your own primary texts

- [10 Minute Tutorial: Recogito (Pelagios Network)](https://recogito.pelagios.org/documentation/getting-started) - A guide to semantic annotation, person/place tagging, and relationship-mapping across digitized texts and images.
- [Introduction to Encoding Texts in TEI (Programming Historian)](https://programminghistorian.org/en/lessons/introduction-to-tei-1) - A practical tutorial on marking up historical documents with XML-based Text Encoding Initiative (TEI) standards.
- [How to Prepare an Annotated Bibliography (Cornell University Library)](https://guides.library.cornell.edu/annotatedbibliography) - Instructions for summarizing, critically evaluating, and establishing the historiographical relevance of primary sources.

## Build your own interactive timelines

- [TimelineJS: Make a Timeline (Knight Lab)](https://timeline.knightlab.com/) - An open-source tool and template workflow for converting chronological source spreadsheets into interactive multimedia narratives.
- [Making a Timeline from a Google Spreadsheet (Knight Lab)](https://timeline.knightlab.com/docs/using-spreadsheets.html) - Documentation covering date formatting, era spans, media embeds, and event grouping for historical chronology projects.

## Record your own history podcasts

- [Starting Your Podcast: A Guide for Students (NPR)](https://www.npr.org/2018/11/15/662070097/starting-your-podcast-a-guide-for-students) - A blueprint for producing narrative audio, covering story structure, scripting, interview techniques, and field recording.
- [Project Audio: Teaching Students How to Produce Their Own Podcasts (The New York Times)](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/04/19/learning/lesson-plans/project-audio-teaching-students-how-to-produce-their-own-podcasts.html) - A structured project unit breaking down voiceover narration, audio editing, and sound design workflows.

## Publish your own public history essays

- [Writing for Public-Facing Digital History Publications: Some Tips & Tricks (The Panorama)](https://thepanorama.shear.org/2024/07/08/writing-for-public-facing-digital-history-publications/) - Guidance from journal editors on translating scholarly arguments into accessible, clear essays for non-academic readers.
- [Writing History for a Popular Audience: A Round Table (The American Historian / OAH)](https://www.oah.org/tah/issues/2016/november/writing-history-for-a-popular-audience-a-round-table/) - Practicing historians evaluate narrative craft, evidence presentation, and stylistic clarity outside the academy.

## Write your own scholarly book reviews

- [How to Write a History Book Review (University of Iowa History Department)](https://history.uiowa.edu/resources/undergraduates/writing-guides/book-review) - A guide covering thesis identification, source evaluation, and assessing how a monograph contributes to broader historical debates.
- [Book Reviews (UNC Writing Center)](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/book-reviews/) - Structural blueprints and critical questions for evaluating nonfiction arguments and scholarly evidence.
- [How to Write an Academic Book Review (Wendy Laura Belcher)](https://www.wendybelcher.com/writing-advice/how-to-write-book-review/) - A professional step-by-step drafting schedule, outlining formula, and evaluation model by an academic editor.
- [How to Write a Critical Book Review (Carleton College History Department)](https://www.carleton.edu/history/resources/history-study-guides/how-to-write-a-critical-book-review/) - Specific criteria for examining a historian's methodological approach, use of primary evidence, and underlying interpretive bias.

## Draft your own historiographical research papers

- [Reading, Writing, and Researching for History: A Guide for College Students (Patrick Rael, Bowdoin College)](https://courses.bowdoin.edu/writing-guides/) - A full guide to the research lifecycle, from interrogating sources and predatory reading to crafting historiographical interventions.
- [Steps for Writing a History Paper (UCLA Department of History)](https://history.ucla.edu/steps-for-writing-a-history-paper/) - A sequential workflow moving from topic selection and source interrogation to drafting and thesis argumentation.
- [How to Write a History Research Paper (Carleton College History Department)](https://www.carleton.edu/history/resources/history-research-paper/) - Walkthrough on structuring an extended historical research paper, outlining thematic sections, and managing citations.
- [A Brief Guide to Writing the History Paper (Harvard College Writing Center)](https://hwpi.harvard.edu/files/hwp/files/bg_writing_history.pdf) - Concise guidance on constructing evidence-backed historical claims, evaluating scholarly disagreements, and avoiding anachronism.
